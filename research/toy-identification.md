# Toy identification

## The one the user calls "playmate fun to drive"

**Playmates "Fun-to-Drive" Corvette Dashboard, 1985, made in Hong Kong.**
Sold in North America; common on eBay/Reddit as a nostalgia item.

Distinguishing features vs the Tomy (per Nostalgia Nerd, who owned one):

- **Pop-up headlights** on the front
- **Screen directly in front of the steering wheel** (the Tomy's screen sits
  beside the wheel)
- Red **Corvette-ish** sports-car dashboard styling
- 4 × D-cell batteries; battery door notoriously tight
- Very loud motor noise, "especially in high gear" → it has a **2-position
  HI/LO gear shift** like the Tomy
- The printed road loop **repeats about every 2 seconds**
- Per the same owner: on the Playmates unit **you could not actually crash**
  — steering moves the road, hazards pass by, but no crash end-state (unlike
  the Tomy, which has a defined crash/off-road game-over)

Images: `images/playmates-1985/`

## The archetype: Tomy Turnin' Turbo Dashboard (1983)

- Child-sized sports-car **dashboard replica** with working parts and a
  windshield-style viewing screen showing a fixed car on a winding road
- Controls: **steering wheel, gear shift, ignition key** (on/off), plus
  speedometer / tachometer / fuel gauge on the dashboard face
- Front fascia: **headlights with a bumper** below
- Electromechanical, not video: a **lamp + rotating drum** "projector" system
  (see `toy-mechanics.md`), 4 × D batteries
- Also released/cloned as "Racing Turbo" (Europe) and others

Images: `images/tomy-1983/` (including the period **schematic diagram**)

## The Brazilian variant: Tectoy Turbo Video Driver (1991)

- Made by **Tectoy** (Sega's Brazilian partner, founded 1987 specifically to
  make high-tech toys) during its "hybrid mechanical + electronic toys" phase
- Brazilian sellers describe: **volante (wheel), câmbio (gear shift), chave de
  ignição (ignition key), painel (dashboard)**
- A 2026 auction listing confirms it still uses a **physically moving track**
  ("funcionando luzes, som e movimento da pista"), i.e. the same drum/belt
  mechanism, plus lights and sound
- Approx. size: **15 × 25 × 25 cm**
- Listed in Tectoy's product history as "Turbo Driver"; actively nostalgia-
  marketed by Tectoy's own social media

Images: `images/tectoy-1991/`

## Why this matters for the game

The project name says "playmate", and the reference shell should follow the
**Playmates Corvette** silhouette (pop-up lights, center-mounted screen), while
the **game rules** we mimic come from the Tomy (rolling road, hazards, score,
fuel, game-over) because they are the best documented and match the user's
description ("static car image with a rolling background... avoiding hazards").
The Tectoy variant confirms the formula survived into 90s Brazil and gives us
a compact real-world size reference for the 3D model.
